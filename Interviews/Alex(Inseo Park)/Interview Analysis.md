# Interview Analysis Report: UQ Campus Heritage & Student Belonging

## 1. Participant Overview
* **Participant:** Kyle Shelman (UQ Student)
* **Interviewer:** Inseo Park (Master of Interaction Design)
---

## 2. Qualitative Analysis by Domain

### Q1: Physical Touchpoints & Awareness
* **User Response:** Noticed natural and architectural spaces like the lab building’s skylight area with rocks and plants, but didn't recall specific historical markers or statues.
* **UX Insight:** Students interact with campus environments visually and spatially, but physical markers need clearer cues to communicate cultural or historical significance.

### Q2 & Q3: Exploration & Social Sharing
* **User Response:** Has not actively learned about campus spaces through university channels. Conversations back home focus on routine student activities (e.g., classes).
* **UX Insight:** Campus heritage is currently absent from daily peer-to-peer storytelling. Creating memorable campus micro-moments can encourage narrative sharing.

### Q4: Historical Architecture Perception
* **User Response:** Views iconic buildings (e.g., Forgan Smith) mainly as background scenery due to time constraints ("usually too busy").
* **UX Insight:** Information delivery must be quick and ambient. Static architecture requires active, low-effort triggers to spark curiosity during student commutes.

### Q5: Design Opportunities & Student Preferences
* **User Response:** Expressed interest in interactive museum-like formats (e.g., pressing a button for audio playback) in high-traffic campus spots.
* **UX Insight:** Interactive, physical-digital touchpoints that offer audio storytelling provide an accessible and engaging way to deliver campus culture without overburdening students.

---

## 3. Interaction Design Recommendations
1. **Phygital Story Triggers:** Place physical interaction points (buttons, NFC, or audio triggers) at key transit areas.
2. **Audio-First Micro-Content:** Keep stories short (under 30 seconds) to accommodate busy student schedules.
3. **Context-Aware Installations:** Integrate cultural narratives directly into well-frequented natural and lab spaces.
