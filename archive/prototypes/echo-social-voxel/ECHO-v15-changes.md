# v15 — what changed

Two builds this round, from the same code. Everything below is in both.

## 1. The demo profile survives being reopened

**The bug:** picking *Show me a full profile* filled LAKEWALKER-07 with
Echoes, saves and alerts — but closing the pod and tapping *That's me, carry
on* gave you an empty account. The simulated half (other people's answers,
their likes, the alerts those produced) was never written down, so it was gone
on the next load.

**The fix:** the demo account is rebuilt from its seed whenever it is resumed.
Same eleven Echoes, same six saved threads, same answers from the same people,
same twenty-four alerts with the same four unread. Nothing drifts.

## 2. Alerts that actually say what happened

The Alerts tab was a list of one-liners. It now reads as a feed:

- who it was, as an avatar — purple for an answer, red for a like;
- **what they did** — *GUMNUT-30 added to your Echo*;
- **what they said**, quoted, when it was an answer;
- which of your Echoes, and which thread;
- how long ago, with the unread ones highlighted and counted at the top.

Tapping one opens your Echo on your tree, with their answer under it. This is
the loop the whole pod is for: leave something, come back, see who picked it
up — so it is now the part that shows the most.

The **menu** carries the count too, so you can see something has happened
before you go looking for it.

## 3. A second build: the block garden

`ECHO-social-voxel/` is the same app with the garden redrawn as an isometric
world of cubes — a block island, blocky trees, pixel flowers, names on plates.

The reason is navigation. In the painted garden the trees stand in a row and
the ones behind are hidden. The block garden opens on the whole island, every
tree on its own ground, nothing behind anything else. It is also, frankly, more
fun to move around in, which matters for a thing people walk up to once.

The pixel flower runs through everything it should: on the trees, in the
share → flower → your tree animation, and on the confirmation card.

The artwork is generated by the app — cubes, plates and pixel squares drawn in
code. No texture pack, no game's assets.
