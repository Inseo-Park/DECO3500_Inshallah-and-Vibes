# ECHO — Place-Based Storytelling Installation

> **DECO3500 — Inshallah and Vibes**  
> **University of Queensland (UQ)**  
> **Project:** ECHO — Stories, memories and moments left behind for others to discover

## 1. Project Overview

ECHO is a place-based storytelling experience designed to help students connect with UQ’s history, campus identity and one another through short, optional interactions.

The project combines:

- A physical cardboard prototype with an integrated touchscreen.
- A digital interface for discovering and sharing Echoes.
- Historical stories connected to UQ locations.
- Memories and experiences contributed by students and community members.
- Audio, video, text and visual storytelling.
- An asynchronous social interaction where one person can leave an Echo for another person to discover later.

The experience is designed to encourage curiosity, belonging and low-pressure participation. Users can choose whether to listen, watch, read, reflect or leave something behind.

## 5. Current Prototype

The current ECHO prototype includes:

1. An ECHO landing screen.
2. An explanation of the Echo concept.
3. Listen and Discover and Share Something pathways.
4. Story-category selection.
5. Audio, video and text-based storytelling.
6. Reflection and feedback questions.
7. An optional contribution pathway.
8. A physical cardboard prototype containing a touchscreen.

The physical prototype represents the proposed book-like installation. It was created to explore how the physical form and digital interface work together as one storytelling experience.

The prototype and documentation demonstrate the project’s development through research, ideation, testing and design iteration.

## 7. Physical Prototype

The physical prototype is a handmade cardboard installation with a tablet positioned inside a book-like structure.

The prototype explores:

- How the physical form attracts attention.
- How a screen can be integrated into a physical storytelling object.
- Whether the installation feels approachable and comfortable.
- How users move between the physical object and the digital experience.
- How a quiet, individual interaction can support place-based storytelling.

The physical prototype is documented through photographs in the `Week 9 Stand Up/` folder.

---

## 8. Storytelling Content

ECHO includes stories and experiences connected to UQ and its campus history.

One of the developed storytelling directions focuses on student activism during the Vietnam War era and the 1970s and 1980s. The story explores:

- Student activism and civil liberties.
- Anti-war and anti-conscription movements.
- Protests and the right to march.
- Anti-apartheid and Indigenous rights activism.
- Music, student culture and alternative media.
- The relationship between universities, students and political expression.

The storytelling experience uses a combination of narration, historical context, visual material and reflective questions.

---

## 9. Testing and Evaluation

The project was evaluated through student feedback and prototype testing.

The testing explored:

- Whether students are curious about UQ’s history.
- Where students are likely to encounter the experience.
- How much time students are willing to spend interacting.
- Preferences for audio, video, text and mixed media.
- Whether the physical installation feels approachable.
- The importance of privacy and personal comfort.
- Whether users would be interested in discovering or leaving an Echo.

Feedback from testing informed the project’s design decisions, including the smaller book-like physical form, placement in familiar campus spaces, short interactions and flexible storytelling formats.
