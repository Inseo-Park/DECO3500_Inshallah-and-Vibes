# Secondary Research

## Literature Analysis

### 1. Student belonging and relationships

[School Belonging: The Importance of Student and Teacher Relationships](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-64537-3_21)

This chapter shows that student belonging is very important and provides great benefits when relationships are built and students feel connected to their institution.

### 2. Cultural heritage, place attachment and cultural identity

[Legacy of culture heritage building revitalization: Place attachment and culture identity](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1314223)

Traditional curriculum approaches in architectural and higher education settings frequently neglect historical places, libraries, and heritage buildings as living knowledge sources, resulting in low cultural awareness and weak student engagement with their physical built environment. Without knowledge transfer regarding the cultural identity, historical memory, and significance of campus buildings and architectural heritage, students struggle to develop "place-belonging" or emotional attachment to their physical surroundings.

### 3. Universities, loneliness and a sense of home

[The university as sanctuary: Home and unhomeliness](https://doi.org/10.1093/jopedu/qhae037)

This article shows that students feel lonely when universities only design campuses for functional and educational purposes rather than providing some form of historical value.

### 4. Campus history and a sense of place

[Puke Ahu: Articulating a place-based, university campus identity](https://doi.org/10.1080/1177083X.2016.1261909)

- **Campus history can be invisible** to current students and staff.
- A university is more than buildings; it has **cultural, political, Indigenous and geographical histories**.
- Learning about a place's history can strengthen **sense of place and belonging**.
- **Multiple perspectives** are important; campus history shouldn't be presented as one official story.
- Indigenous history should be **embedded in the understanding of place**, not treated as an add-on.
- **Walking through physical spaces** can become part of learning about their history.
- Existing historical resources don't guarantee engagement; **time and accessibility are barriers**.
- Key idea: **"thicken" the campus** by revealing the stories and histories behind familiar places.

### 5. Historical trails and physical exploration

[A heritage 'trailscape': Tracking the exploits of historical figures – an Australian case study](https://doi.org/10.1080/1743873X.2016.1242593)

Looks at how historical trails connect people with places associated with historical figures. Useful for understanding **how physical movement through a landscape can become a historical experience**. The author is from **UQ**.
