# Campus Echoes — User Testing Results

## Overview

This page summarises interaction data collected from **10 prototype testing sessions** conducted on **16 September 2026**.

The testing explored how participants interacted with the Campus Echoes experience, including:

- whether they chose to **listen to an Echo** or **share an Echo**
- which type of historical content they were curious about
- whether they engaged first with **video or audio**
- their reactions to the content
- their preferred media format
- whether they contributed their own Echo
- where participants stopped or completed the interaction flow

> **Note:** This was a small prototype test. Not every participant reached every question, so later-stage preference results should be interpreted as qualitative indications rather than representative statistics.

| Metric | Result |

## Entry Behaviour

The majority of participants initially chose to **discover/listen to existing stories** rather than immediately contribute their own.

| Entry Choice | Participants |
|---|---:|
| Listen | **8 (80%)** |
| Share | **2 (20%)** |

### Interpretation

This suggests that **discovery is an important entry point** for Campus Echoes. Participants generally appeared more willing to explore existing content before contributing their own story.

Contribution should therefore remain easy to access, but the experience should not depend on users being ready to share immediately.

---

## Curiosity Choices

Among the **8 participants who chose Listen**, curiosity selections were:

| Curiosity Category | Participants |
|---|---:|
| Surprise | **3** |
| Place | **3** |
| Moment | **2** |

The choices were relatively distributed, suggesting that there was interest across multiple ways of discovering UQ history rather than one dominant category.

### Subtopic Choices

Where an additional subtopic was selected:

- **Place:** participants selected **“Something people don't know”**
- **Moment:** participants selected **“Something from the past”**
- **Surprise:** the prototype selected content directly

This supports retaining multiple discovery pathways rather than forcing users into a single chronological or location-based browsing method.

---

## Media Engagement

Of the **8 participants who reached a story**:

| First Media Selected | Participants |
|---|---:|
| Video | **5 (62.5%)** |
| Audio | **3 (37.5%)** |

Video was the more common first choice.

### Video

Three participants progressed far enough to provide a reaction to the video, and all three selected:

> **Curious**

Additional comments included:

> “Add subtitles.”

> “We don’t know a lot about the history of UQ; this video made it more accessible.”

Another participant described the video as more impressive and interesting.

Of the participants who later answered the format preference question:

| Preferred Format | Responses |
|---|---:|
| Video | **2** |
| Both video and audio | **1** |
| Audio | **0** |

One participant explained:

> “I’m a visual person and the images grabbed my attention.”

### Audio

Audio produced mixed reactions:

- one participant selected **Connected**
- one participant selected **Unmoved**
- no complete audio playback was recorded during the sessions

Because only a small number of participants provided an audio reaction, this is **not enough evidence to conclude that audio should be removed**. However, the prototype should investigate how audio is introduced and supported visually.

## Contribution Behaviour

A total of **4 participants left an Echo**.

Importantly, only **2 participants chose Share at the beginning**, while some participants who initially chose Listen later contributed an Echo.

This suggests that **exploring existing stories can encourage later participation**.

### Contribution Format

| Echo Format | Participants |
|---|---:|
| Written | **2** |
| Recorded | **2** |

There was an even split between written and recorded contributions.

### Contribution Type

| Echo Type | Participants |
|---|---:|
| Event | **2** |
| Fact | **1** |
| Secret | **1** |

The variety of contribution types indicates that participants interpreted an “Echo” broadly, including both historical information and personal experiences.

One written contribution referenced attending a recent UQ event with a friend, demonstrating that users may contribute **current campus memories as well as older historical stories**.

## Key Findings

### 1. Users primarily wanted to explore before contributing

**80%** of participants selected Listen at the beginning.

However, participants who initially explored the experience could still move on to contribute their own Echo.

**Design implication:** make content discovery the main onboarding experience while keeping the contribution action visible throughout the journey.

---

### 2. Video generated the strongest positive feedback

Video was selected first by **5 of the 8 listeners**, and every participant who reached the video reaction question selected **Curious**.

Participants specifically mentioned visual engagement and improved accessibility to UQ history.

**Design implication:** continue using short visual stories as a major content format.

---

### 3. Subtitles should be added to video content

A participant explicitly requested:

> “Add subtitles.”

Subtitles would also improve accessibility in noisy campus environments and for users who prefer or require text alongside spoken content.

**Design implication:** provide captions/subtitles by default for video stories.

---

### 4. Audio needs further iteration

Audio produced both a positive (**Connected**) and negative (**Unmoved**) reaction, while no full audio playback was recorded.

**Design implication:** investigate shorter audio clips, stronger visual accompaniment, clearer duration indicators, transcripts, or previews before playback.

---

### 5. Users were willing to contribute in multiple formats

Among participants who left an Echo:

- **2 wrote an Echo**
- **2 recorded an Echo**

There was therefore no clear preference between writing and recording in this small sample.

**Design implication:** retain both contribution methods rather than forcing users into one format.

---

### 6. Campus Echoes can support both history and living memory

Participant contributions were not limited to distant historical events. At least one participant contributed a recent campus experience.

**Design implication:** position Campus Echoes as a platform for both **discovering UQ history** and **building an ongoing record of campus life**.

---

## Usability Issues Identified

### Long periods before interaction

The median time to first interaction was only **6.8 seconds**, but a small number of sessions recorded much longer delays.

These may reflect participants reading, receiving testing instructions, discussing the prototype, or pausing rather than a direct interface problem.

**Action:** do not rely on average time alone when assessing landing-page usability. Observe participants alongside telemetry during future tests.

### Drop-off before later questions

Only:

- 5 participants reached Second Pass
- 4 reached Compare
- 3 reached Takeaway

This means the later parts of the experience had substantially fewer responses.

**Action:** reduce unnecessary steps and reconsider whether every reaction/comparison question is required before users can continue.

### Media accessibility

The request for subtitles indicates that media should not rely entirely on sound.

**Action:** include subtitles for video and transcripts or supporting text for audio.

---

## Design Changes Informed by Testing

Based on these sessions, the next prototype iteration should:

1. **Prioritise story discovery** on entry while keeping Share easily accessible.
2. **Retain multiple curiosity pathways** such as Place, Moment and Surprise.
3. **Keep video as a major storytelling format.**
4. **Add subtitles/captions to video.**
5. **Improve audio presentation**, potentially through shorter clips, transcripts and stronger visual context.
6. **Retain both written and recorded Echo contributions.**
7. **Allow users to contribute after listening**, as discovery appears capable of encouraging participation.
8. **Review the length of the post-story flow** to reduce drop-off before Compare and Takeaway screens.
9. **Support recent memories as well as historical stories**, allowing the collection to grow over time.

---

## Limitations

These findings should be interpreted within the scope of the prototype test:

- only **10 sessions** were recorded
- not every participant completed the full interaction flow
- some participants may have paused while receiving instructions or speaking with researchers
- later-stage questions have a much smaller response count
- all Listen sessions in this dataset were served the same Echo (`027`), limiting comparison between different story content
- behavioural telemetry indicates what participants did, but does not always explain why they did it

Future testing should include a larger number of participants, multiple Echoes, and short follow-up interviews to combine behavioural data with qualitative explanations.

---

## Overall Takeaway

The testing indicates that Campus Echoes works most naturally as a **discovery-first experience**: users initially explore stories about UQ and may then be encouraged to contribute memories of their own.

Video received the clearest positive feedback, while audio requires further refinement. The contribution flow successfully supported both written and recorded stories, and user behaviour suggests that Campus Echoes can function not only as a way to access university history, but also as a way for the UQ community to continuously add to that history.
